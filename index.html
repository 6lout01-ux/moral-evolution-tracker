Behind the test

The thinking behind it.

A person's actions can reflect both a relatively stable disposition and the situation they find themselves in. This tracker is designed to show that tension rather than hide it.

What counts as “evil” here?

For this project, evil means repeatedly and knowingly harming, exploiting, or discriminating against others when less harmful alternatives are available. One bad decision is not enough to establish a person's character.

Why two rounds?

The first round provides a baseline. The second introduces situational pressures, such as obeying a boss, being watched by peers, or believing an action will remain anonymous. Comparing the rounds helps reveal where choices are sensitive to context.

Why keep a log?

Class 6 discusses Walter Mischel's cognitive-affective processing system, including variations in behavior across situations. Repeated observations can reveal if-then patterns. A log also lets someone document accountability and change instead of being judged by their worst moment.

Scoring, clearly explained

Each response receives an illustrative value between 0 and 100 based on the amount of deliberate harm and protection of others. A category score is the mean of its answers; the overall assessment uses weighted categories.

The weighting and cutoffs were designed for this classroom prototype. They are not empirically validated and should not be used to diagnose, rank, hire, punish, or label real people.
