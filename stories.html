Methodology

How good and evil are defined.

This test examines repeated choices, the situations surrounding them, and the balance between preventing harm and knowingly causing it.

Mostly good

A mostly good pattern means consistently choosing empathy, fairness, honesty, resistance to harmful authority, and responsibility for mistakes, especially when doing so has a personal cost.

Mostly evil

A mostly evil pattern means repeatedly choosing harmful, exploitative, dishonest, or discriminatory actions despite less harmful alternatives, often for personal gain or under social pressure. A single mistake is not enough to establish a pattern.

Mixed / undetermined

A score of exactly 50 lies on the dividing line and is not classified as either mostly good or mostly evil.

One score, two components

Animated portion (30%): Four branching stories, each completed twice, for eight scored decisions.

Written portion (70%): Nine dilemmas answered in baseline and pressured conditions, for 18 scored decisions across five categories.

Combined score = 70% × written category-weighted average + 30% × animated response average, rounded to the nearest integer.

Scores of 51–100 = Mostly Good, 0–49 = Mostly Evil, and 50 = Mixed / Undetermined. All 26 decisions must be completed before a final classification appears.

These scores and labels are educational rather than scientifically validated; they should not be used for real-world moral judgments or psychological diagnosis.

Why pressure and repeated observations matter

People may behave differently when authority figures, group expectations, anonymity, or rewards change what is at stake. The six-week check-in log records later behavior separately, but those self-ratings do not alter the initial morality score.
