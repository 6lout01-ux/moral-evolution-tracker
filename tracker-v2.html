Understanding the tracker

Why patterns matter.

A person's actions can reflect both a relatively stable disposition and the situation they find themselves in. This tracker is designed to show that tension rather than hide it.

What counts as “evil” here?

For this tracker, harmful behavior means repeatedly and knowingly harming, exploiting, or discriminating against others when less harmful alternatives are available. One bad decision is not enough to establish a person's character.

Why two rounds?

The first round provides a baseline. The second introduces situational pressures, such as obeying a boss, being watched by peers, or believing an action will remain anonymous. Comparing the rounds helps reveal where choices are sensitive to context.

Why keep a log?

Behavior can change with circumstances. Tracking specific situations over multiple weeks makes it easier to recognize patterns, such as when someone speaks up, stays silent, or repairs a mistake. A log also leaves room for accountability and improvement.

Scoring, clearly explained

Each response receives an illustrative value between 0 and 100 based on the amount of deliberate harm and protection of others. A category score is the mean of its answers; the overall assessment uses weighted categories.

The weights are illustrative and have not been scientifically validated. Scores should not be used to diagnose, rank, hire, punish, or label real people.
